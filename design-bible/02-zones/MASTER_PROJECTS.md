## §1 · What Projects OWNS
- **The proof.** The artifacts the Maker has built — the concrete evidence behind every claim
  made elsewhere in the World.
- **The hiring decision.** This is the zone where "maybe" becomes "yes." Everything before is
  promise; this is the receipt.
- **The touchable demonstration** of the build half of the identity (consumer + enterprise).

## §2 · What Projects does NOT own
- **Capabilities / transformation** → the **Forest** ("what I can do," "how I became a builder").
- **Professional record / where the work happened** → the **Mountains** ("where I've done it").
- **Who the Maker is** → the **Hero House.**
- **How the Maker thinks / values** → the **Village.**
- **Ambition / call-to-action** → the **Future.**
- **The meta-claim "I built this world"** → this is the **Project Zero meta-reveal** (§11),
  which lives at the **Summit**, *not* as a room in this zone.

> **The Mountains↔Projects wall (most re-merge-prone):** the Mountains give **context** (role,
> scope, outcomes) and *portal out* to here; Projects give the **artifact.** A Mountain chamber
> that shows the work in depth is stealing this zone's job, and vice-versa.

## §3 · Why the Projects Zone exists
Because a beautiful world that makes a recruiter *feel* and *like* and *believe* still has not
made them *act* — and action requires undeniable proof. Projects exists to convert conviction
into a hire. It is the single highest-leverage zone (Bible §11): the Forest makes them curious;
Projects gets them hired.

## §4 · Recruiter takeaway
After Projects, the recruiter holds **conviction + specificity + the urge to act**: *"This
person is real. I can see exactly what they'd bring. I want to talk to them."* Not "nice work" —
a concrete sense of capability and a readiness for the Future zone's call-to-action.

## §5 · The four balances (the zone's tuning)
Projects is tuned *differently from the Forest* because its job is decision, not reframe:

- **Story-led vs evidence-led → ~60/40, story-led but evidence-anchored.** Lead with the human
  problem and the insight (makes them care); always include role, what was built, and outcome
  (makes them believe). Pure story = unconvincing; pure case-study = unread.
- **Interaction vs observation → mixed, by world.** The flagship (Jarvis) is *genuinely
  interactive* (touch it). The impact story (Agents) is *guided observation* with a light
  trigger. Not every world must be heavily interactive — that's unsustainable and dilutes the
  flagship.
- **Immersion vs usability → usability-leaning (the key inversion).** Unlike the Forest
  (immersion-leaning), Projects must let a recruiter *extract value fast.* Immersion *frames*
  the work; usability *delivers* it. When the two conflict here, usability wins.
- **Exploration vs efficiency → efficiency-leaning.** This is where the **Fast Path** lands.
  Reaching the proof must be quick and non-linear; exploration is a bonus, never a toll.

## §6 · The recruiter journey
- **Before entering:** primed and *wanting* — the Village (likeable), House (connected), Forest
  (reframed) have built enough trust that seeing the work feels like a *reward*, not a chore.
  Anticipation, not obligation.
- **While exploring:** fast orientation to "what is this and what did it do," then a choice to go
  deeper. The flagship invites a *try it*; the impact story invites a *watch it resolve.* At no
  point are they confused about what they're looking at or how to engage.
- **After leaving:** conviction and momentum — handed directly toward the Future's
  call-to-action. They can name what the Maker builds and why few others combine these abilities.

## §7 · Emotional / narrative / experience journeys
- **Emotional:** anticipation → curiosity → delight (Jarvis) → respect (Agents) → conviction →
  momentum-to-act.
- **Narrative:** the destination of the coming-of-age arc — *here is what the builder builds.*
  The proof that the journey's promises were true.
- **Experience:** arrive → orient fast → choose a world → engage (touch / watch) → reach a clear
  "this is real" beat → exit toward the Future. Hub-and-spoke; no forced sequence between worlds.

## §8 · Relationships
- **Village:** the Village hub provides **direct access** to Projects — the primary destination
  of the Fast Path. A time-poor recruiter reaches the proof from the hub without traversing the
  World.
- **Hero House:** the House makes the recruiter *care about the person*; Projects then makes them
  *believe in the work.* Emotion first, evidence second.
- **Forest:** the Forest *performs* the capabilities; Projects *proves* them with artifacts. The
  Forest's **Story Fire** shows the Maker's real reel — content that is *sourced from* this zone.
- **Mountains:** each Mountain Gate's Context Chamber **portals out** to the relevant Project
  World ("built here"). Mountains = context; Projects = artifact. Cross-linked, never merged.
- **Future:** Projects hands the recruiter — now convinced — directly to the Future's
  call-to-action. Projects creates the urge; the Future captures it.
- **Fast Path:** Projects is the Fast Path's home. The Fast Path must always be able to deliver a
  rushed visitor straight here.

## §9 · The Project Worlds — structure
- **Two featured Project Worlds:** **Jarvis** (interactive flagship) and **The Agent Suite**
  (impact story). Both prove the *build* half of the identity — consumer and enterprise scale.
- **Project Zero is NOT a Project World.** It is a **meta-reveal** (§11) at the Summit; it also
  carries the *design/experience-craft* proof (the whole world as evidence of design taste).
- **Cap: three.** A third Project World is permitted *only* if a future built artifact
  independently clears the Tier-1 bar (real, showable, distinct facet). Default and current
  state is **two.** Memory over completeness governs — two immaculate worlds beat three padded.
- **Each world proves a distinct facet of the one identity.** No two worlds may make the same
  point.

## §10 · Per-world architecture

### JARVIS — the interactive flagship
- **Purpose:** prove the Maker *engineers sophisticated, beautiful, novel tools from scratch* —
  Creative *and* Technologist at once.
- **Narrative role:** the touchable proof; the artifact the recruiter handles directly.
- **Emotional role:** delight + "I can actually use this."
- **Recruiter role:** undeniable hands-on evidence of build + craft; **backstops the unshowable
  Agent Suite** (its provable engineering lends credibility to the agents).
- **Interaction model:** *genuinely interactive* — a guided "try it" (speak a command, watch
  fuzzy/phonetic matching forgive a misheard word, launch a workspace). Trivial-not-frustrating
  (`INTERACTION_PHILOSOPHY §4`); a try, not a tutorial.
- **Environmental storytelling:** the tool *is* the exhibit, framed by the world; the real
  interface presented as a usable artifact (`ENVIRONMENTAL_STORYTELLING §6` — evidence anchor).
- **Success criteria:** the recruiter uses it, believes it's real and impressive, and registers
  it was hand-engineered.
- **Failure modes:** reads as a fake mockup; too complex to grasp; not actually interactive;
  drowned in explanatory text; the engineering depth invisible.

### THE AGENT SUITE — the impact story
- **Purpose:** prove the Maker *builds production AI systems real teams depend on* — the
  differentiator (a design background shipping enterprise agents is rare).
- **Narrative role:** the impact/scale proof; enterprise weight.
- **Emotional role:** respect — "this person ships real, useful intelligence."
- **Recruiter role:** the differentiator evidence + business literacy; completes the "designer →
  builds production AI" reframe.
- **Interaction model:** *guided observation with a light trigger* — the recruiter triggers a
  scenario and **watches the logic resolve** (e.g., a ticket closes → rotation selects a
  reviewer → skips the person on leave → notifies). DIRECT-adjacent, observation-led. Dummy data;
  the live systems are behind corporate walls.
- **Environmental storytelling:** an operations space where the agents' work is made **visible as
  flowing, resolving processes**; anchored to sanitized real outputs where possible.
- **Success criteria:** the recruiter grasps what the agents do and that they are real and used;
  the rarity of the achievement lands.
- **Failure modes:** so abstract/confidential it reads as vaporware; over-claims; indistinguishable
  from a generic AI demo; too much text; the "real teams depend on this" weight not felt.

## §11 · Project Zero — the meta-reveal (not a Project World)
- **Definition:** the explicit *claiming* of the medium-is-the-message thesis (Bible §0) — *"you
  have just walked through one of my projects."* It is a **realization placed at a charged
  moment**, never a room you enter.
- **Primary home:** the **Summit** (Mountains) — the present-self culmination. Reinforced as a
  quiet through-line across the World (the responsive world + self-portrait already plant it).
- **Dual duty:** it also serves as the **design / experience-craft proof** — the entire world is
  the evidence of design taste — which is why the Projects zone needs no separate design world.
- **Success criteria:** the recruiter *consciously registers* "he built ALL of this," which
  retroactively elevates every prior zone.
- **Failure modes:** stated too early or too flatly (anticlimax); built as a recursive room
  (hall of mirrors); or never claimed at all (the recruiter misses the single biggest proof).

## §12 · What BELONGS / what does NOT
**Belongs:** a genuinely usable flagship; a guided agent simulation that resolves visibly; story
that leads with a human problem and lands on an outcome; fast orientation; clear "your role" and
real outcomes (filled later); hub-and-spoke access; the Project Zero reveal at the Summit.

**Does NOT belong:** a Behance grid; case-study walls of text; more than three worlds; two worlds
making the same point; a Project World that's beautiful but not believable; career history
(Mountains' job); a Project Zero "room"; forced linear traversal; immersion that costs the
recruiter speed; metrics/achievements faked to fill space.

## §13 · Pressure-test record (what was challenged and decided)
- **Project Zero as a peer Project World → REJECTED.** Category error/recursion; nothing new to
  enter; its power is a realization, not an exhibit; it's a different *kind* of thing. → Became a
  **meta-reveal at the Summit** that also carries the design-craft proof.
- **Backfilling a third "creative production / design" world → REJECTED (for now).** The Project
  Zero reveal already carries the design-craft proof, so a third room would violate *memory over
  completeness.* Cap remains three, default two; a third must independently earn Tier-1.
- **Jarvis as flagship → CONFIRMED.** Touchable, hand-engineered, proves both identity halves,
  fully showable; backstops the Agents' credibility.
- **Agent Suite as impact story → CONFIRMED.** The differentiator; shown as guided simulation
  because the live systems are confidential.
- **Immersion-leaning (Forest model) for Projects → REJECTED.** Projects is the decision zone →
  **usability-leaning.**

## §14 · Scope protection
- ❌ No more than three Project Worlds, ever. Default two.
- ❌ No Project World that duplicates another's facet.
- ❌ No Project Zero "room"; it stays a meta-reveal at the Summit.
- ❌ No career history, responsibilities, or company context here (Mountains).
- ❌ No case-study text walls, Behance grids, or résumé content.
- ❌ No forced linear order between worlds; hub-and-spoke only.
- ❌ No immersion that taxes recruiter speed (usability wins ties).
- ✅ Allowed without reopening: filling real metrics/screens/copy/outcomes; tuning a world's
  interaction; replacing placeholder evidence with real work; adding a *qualifying* third world
  if it independently clears Tier-1.

## §15 · Quality standards
- Every world clears all `/01-languages` bars (Visual, Material, Lighting, Interaction,
  Environmental Storytelling, Asset).
- The flagship must be *genuinely usable*, not a mockup. The impact story must be *believable*,
  not vaporware.
- Story arc per world: **problem → insight → what I built → outcome.** Legible fast.
- Real artifacts over mockups wherever showable (evidence anchors).
- A recruiter reaches a "this is real" beat in each world within seconds, without reading walls.

## §16 · Success criteria (checkable)
1. The recruiter reaches at least one world and **believes it's real.**
2. They can **name what the Maker builds** after leaving.
3. The flagship is **actually used** (interacted with), not just viewed.
4. The Agents' **rarity/impact** is felt (designer → production AI).
5. They exit with **momentum toward the Future's call-to-action.**
6. A rushed visitor reaches a world **via the Fast Path in seconds.**
