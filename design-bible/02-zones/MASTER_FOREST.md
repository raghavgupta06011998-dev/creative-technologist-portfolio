## 1. Forest narrative

**The Maker's Forest.**

After crossing the bridge, the visitor enters a wild, realistic, cinematic forest that —
slowly — they realize is *tended*. A lantern is lit. The path is cleared and edged with
stones. Someone lives and works here, practicing different crafts in different clearings.
But the visitor never sees them. They only find the **traces of the maker's work**, and
the forest has grown around it, cooperating with it, half-absorbing it. Light behaves a
little strangely near the maker's things. Nature and craft have merged.

The quiet twist, felt and never stated: **the maker is the person whose portfolio this is.**
The entire forest is a self-portrait the visitor walks through without being told. By the
time they reach the Overlook and see the mountains, they understand they have been touring
the inside of one person's mind.

The forest is **freshly inhabited, never abandoned** — warm signs of recent presence
(lit lanterns, a living fire, running water). The emotion is intimacy and curiosity, the
feeling of being let into someone's private working world — never melancholy or ruin.

---

## 2. Why the forest exists

The forest is the bridge between **"this is a warm, real person"** (the village + house)
and **"this person is genuinely capable and worth hiring"** (the project worlds).

It exists to **reframe the visitor's assumption.** They arrive thinking "graphic designer."
They should leave the forest thinking **"this is a designer who builds intelligent
experiences."** That reframe is the forest's only strategic job.

The forest is also the **proof-of-medium**: it is a hand-built, AI-pair-coded, interactive
3D experience. Walking it *is* the demonstration of the headline capability. The medium is
the message.

---

## 3. What the forest communicates

**Personality + ONE headline capability claim. Not a skills list.**

> Headline capability: **"Designer who builds intelligent experiences with AI."**

The forest does **feeling + headline.** It does NOT enumerate tools or skills. Granular
skills (Figma, Tableau, JQL, specific models, etc.) live in **overlay text and the Project
Worlds**, never carved into the forest itself.

The recruiter should NOT think:
> "Wow, he knows Figma, Photoshop, Claude, Jira."

The recruiter SHOULD feel:
> "This person can design, build, automate, see patterns, and tell stories — and I'm
> standing inside something they built. Who is this?"

---

## 4. Recruiter takeaway

What a recruiter remembers after 6 months is brutally small — **one moment + one meta-fact:**

1. **The moment:** *"the swarm of lights that followed where I walked."* (The Living Swarm.)
2. **The meta-fact:** *"that whole world was hand-built in 3D by the designer themselves."*

We do NOT optimize for "communicate all five capabilities." We optimize for **one
unforgettable moment (the Swarm) + one undeniable meta-fact (you built this).** Everything
else is supporting texture that makes those two land.

## 6. Final approved concept

**"The Maker's Forest — a living system that responds to you."**

The forest is one intelligent, hand-built, responsive world. The structure is a **spine +
facets**, NOT five equal monuments — because the maker's real identity is weighted heavily
toward **AI-augmented building**, with design, systems, insight, and storytelling as facets
of *how* they build.

**Core interaction principle:** the recruiter does not *watch* capabilities — they *perform*
them. Each clearing starts **dormant** and the player's single small act **brings it to
life.** Five clearings = five verbs = the recruiter physically rehearses what it is like to
work with this person.

> **REVEAL → DIRECT → CONNECT → SEE → KINDLE**
> (create → orchestrate → systematize → understand → communicate)

**Every clearing must be anchored to at least one piece of real evidence** (real design
work, real agents built, real reel). Magic without evidence = art project. Magic with
evidence = creative technologist. This anchoring is what keeps the forest professional and
legible rather than vague fantasy.

## 7. Forest structure

Sequence along the existing curved forest road (south → north, bridge → mountains):

1. **Entry — The forest notices you.**
   The world subtly reacts to the player's presence (a lantern turns, the path lights
   ahead). Thesis lands before any clearing: *this place is alive and built.*

2. **The Workshop — Design foundation.**
   A tended workshop clearing under a great tree. Grounding opener. Anchored with **real
   design work.** Do not over-invest — it is the foundation, not the headline.

3. **The Living Swarm — AI agents & orchestration. (CENTERPIECE)**
   The differentiator. Largest, most developed, best-lit clearing. Coordinated floating
   lights the player **directs with their movement**. Anchored to the **real agents built
   (Glean, Rovo, ChatGPT, Claude).** This is the moment they remember in 6 months.

4. **The Waterworks — systems, automation, workflows.**
   A hand-built water/routing system that springs to life when the player **connects** one
   gate. Placed **near and visibly linked to the Swarm**, so AI + systems read as one idea:
   *"this is how I build."* This pair is the weighted spine of the forest.

5. **The Overlook — narrative pivot & foresight. (NOT a data clearing)**
   Rise above; **see** the whole connected system you just walked; first clear view of the
   mountains. Foresight + ambition. (Data storytelling does NOT live here — see §8.)

6. **The Story Fire — communication, storytelling & real work.**
   The output clearing. Player **kindles** the fire; firelight shadow-play plays the maker's
   **real reel** — designs, **data-as-story**, presentations. Warmest, final beat.

7. **Transition to the Mountains.**
   The canopy opens, the ground rises, lanterns stop, the climb begins. The exit threshold
   ("Career Mountains Ahead") sits here — the last warm human thing before the ascent.

## 8. Forest experiences (the verbs)

| Clearing | Verb | Player act | Reward | Capability shown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Workshop | **REVEAL** | Choose & develop one rough sketch | Scribble blooms into polished work | Design craft / taste |
| Living Swarm | **DIRECT** | Lead the swarm by where you move | Clearing blooms into coordinated light | AI orchestration / agents |
| Waterworks | **CONNECT** | Flip a stuck gate | Dead machine roars to life | Systems / automation / tooling |
| Overlook | **SEE** | Look through the telescope | Hidden connections + mountains revealed | Insight / foresight / pattern |
| Story Fire | **KINDLE** | Feed the fire | Full vivid shadow-story of real work | Storytelling / data-as-story / communication |

**Connective tissue:**
- **Progressive aliveness** — each clearing starts dormant; the player's act revives it. The
  forest grows brighter and more alive as you advance (the Waterworks literally powers a
  lantern line). This casts the player as the maker, reinforcing the self-portrait.
- **The Overlook payoff** — clearing 5 retroactively reveals the prior acts were one
  connected system. The "aha" that resolves five experiences into one picture.
- **Data storytelling is merged into the Story Fire**, not the Overlook — because data work
  is about turning information into a narrative humans *feel*, which is a storytelling act.

## 9. What was rejected, and why

These were genuinely tried or seriously proposed and are **closed.** Do not reopen without a
major strategic reason.

### Skill trees (glowing cone trees) — REJECTED
- Cartoon/stylized, clashing with the realistic-cinematic art direction.
- A tree that "represents a skill" is decoration with no argument; 26 glowing trees = noise.
- A bare glowing tree shows nothing — the label did all the work.

### Logos / floating logos / logo stations — REJECTED (as the *main* representation)
- A wall of brand logos reads as **"tool collector,"** the exact "I know every tool" energy
  to avoid.
- Logos say "tool user," not "tool master who builds experiences." Different salary band.
- Requires reading; reading breaks immersion and turns the world into a résumé in costume.
- (Real logos may still appear *small, in overlay, or inside Project Worlds* — never as the
  forest's primary visual cue.)

### Rock / monolith monuments — REJECTED
- A rock carries no meaning; a rock + label is just a board with extra steps.
- Added mass without adding message.
- "Monument" is dead stone; the headline capability is *living* orchestration.

### Boards / plaques / signs — REJECTED (as skill representation)
- A board is a label. The moment a visitor reads a wooden sign, they have left the world and
  entered a slideshow.
- Breaks immersion, kills the cinematic mode, makes the forest feel like a classroom/museum.
- (Functional *wayfinding* signage elsewhere in the world is a separate question — this
  rejection is specifically about boards as **skill content** in the forest.)

### Floating holograms / sci-fi node networks (the original "Constellation Orrery") — REJECTED
- Too sci-fi; violates "realistic forest, subtle magic integrated into nature."
- Replaced by **The Living Swarm** (a natural swarm with emergent coordination) — same
  meaning, grounded in nature.

### Floating holographic charts (the original "Observatory") — REJECTED
- Holograms again; banned by the realism constraint.
- Replaced by **The Overlook** (real instruments, reflecting pool, first view of mountains);
  data storytelling moved to the Story Fire.

## 10. Why the forest is NOT a skills section

- A skills section is **read**; the forest is **experienced.** The whole point is to *feel*,
  not to scan a list.
- A skills section enumerates; the forest makes **one headline claim** and lets the projects
  carry the proof.
- A recruiter remembers **one moment**, not a list of 30 items. Optimizing for completeness
  guarantees being forgotten.
- The forest's job is **curiosity + personality + the headline claim.** The **Project Worlds
  get you hired.** The forest must never substitute for finished project work.
- Granular skills/tools belong in **overlay text + Project Worlds.** The forest stays
  sculptural and felt.

## 11. Design principles

1. **Meaning before form.** Never choose an object before locking what it must communicate.
   (This is the rule whose violation caused 6 redesigns.)
2. **Show, don't tell.** No labels as the primary cue. The experience carries the meaning.
3. **Realistic & cinematic.** Golden-hour, semi-realistic, warm. No cartoon, no toy-style.
4. **Subtle magic, integrated into nature.** Any abstract element must read as a natural
   phenomenon (a swarm, firelight, flowing water), never as sci-fi or UI.
5. **Anchor every experience to real evidence.** Magic + artifact. No pure-metaphor clearings.
6. **Personality + one headline, not a capability inventory.**
7. **Weighted to the real identity.** AI-augmented building is the spine; design, systems,
   insight, storytelling are facets.
8. **Connected world.** The forest belongs between the village and the mountains and must
   visually and narratively bridge them.

## 12. Interaction principles

1. **Every clearing has a verb.** The player *performs* the capability; they don't watch it.
2. **Dormant → alive.** Each clearing starts inert and is revived by one small player act.
3. **Trivial, never frustrating.** Interactions are near-obvious (one step, one click, one
   movement). This is a portfolio, not a puzzle game. The reward, not the challenge, is the
   point.
4. **Proximity reactions everywhere.** The world responds to presence — reinforcing
   "responsive experiences I built."
5. **Progress is visible.** Completing a clearing visibly lights/advances the world, pulling
   the player forward.
6. **Provide a fast path.** A persistent, subtle "skip to work / view résumé" affordance for
   rushed recruiters. Immersion for the curious; speed for the busy. (Applies world-wide;
   noted here so the forest never traps a hurried visitor.)

## 13. Performance principles

1. **Procedural for anything that moves/glows/reacts** (swarm, fire shadow-play, water,
   reveals, proximity reactions, all glow). This is the cheap, controllable path.
2. **Downloaded realistic assets only for static physical props** (telescope, waterwheel,
   workbench, totems). Reuse existing forest/campfire assets first.
3. **Shared materials & geometry pools.** One material per role/zone wherever possible.
4. **Instanced systems** for any high-count elements (swarm lights, particles, trees).
5. **No per-element point lights.** Glow is emissive; cap real lights strictly (entrance
   lantern + campfire baseline; day/night system already manages these).
6. **Shadows stay disabled globally** (existing decision — biggest perf win).
7. **Day/Night system already exists** — new emissive materials must register with it so
   night-glow works without new lights.

## 14. Forest Scope Protection

**The forest is LOCKED. To keep it focused, the following must NOT be added later** unless a
major strategic issue is proven:

- ❌ **No new clearings / capabilities.** Five experiences + entry + transition is the whole
  forest. Adding a sixth dilutes the one-moment memory goal.
- ❌ **No skill boards, plaques, signs, or carved text** as skill content. (Wayfinding is a
  separate, non-forest concern.)
- ❌ **No logo walls or floating logo arrays** as primary visuals.
- ❌ **No rock/monolith monuments.**
- ❌ **No tool inventories or "tech stack" displays** in-world.
- ❌ **No sci-fi holograms, HUD panels, or UI-in-the-world.**
- ❌ **No additional tools promoted to their own object.** Tools live in overlay/projects.
- ❌ **No "completeness" additions** ("but we should also show X skill"). Completeness is the
  enemy of memory. The Project Worlds carry depth; the forest carries feeling.
- ❌ **No turning a clearing into a mini-game with real difficulty.** Interactions stay
  trivial and rewarding.
- ❌ **No expanding the forest's job.** It does *curiosity + personality + one headline.* It
  is not the about-me (house), not the proof (projects), not the ambition (mountains).

**Allowed future expansion (does not require reopening the concept):**
- ✅ Swapping/upgrading the realistic assets behind an existing clearing.
- ✅ Tuning glow, motion, color, day/night behavior.
- ✅ Replacing placeholder evidence with real work (designs, agents, reel) as it becomes
  available.
- ✅ Polishing the proximity reactions and progressive-aliveness feedback.
- ✅ Ambient nature enrichment (ferns, mushrooms, light shafts) that serves atmosphere.

## 15. Build order (when implementation begins)

1. **The Living Swarm first** — near-pure procedural, biggest "wow," most on-brand, and the
   boldest test of the "command an intelligence / magic-in-nature" thesis. Build it
   completely, walk it, confirm the feeling **before** building anything else.
2. **The Story Fire** — reuses existing campfire; highest emotional return; showcases real
   work; teases the Project Worlds.
3. **The Overlook**, then **The Workshop**, then **The Waterworks.**

**Honest minimum** if bandwidth tightens: **Swarm + Story Fire + Overlook** (build,
communicate, see-ahead), folding Workshop and Waterworks into smaller dressed clearings.
Three brilliant experiences beat five rushed ones.

**Discipline:** build the Swarm, walk it, confirm — then continue. The form is now locked by
meaning and narrative, so it should stop moving.
