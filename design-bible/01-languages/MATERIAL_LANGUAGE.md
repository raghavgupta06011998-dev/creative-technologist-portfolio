## §1 · The one material sentence
**Real, worn, hand-touched surfaces.** PBR materials that look like they have *aged, been used,
and been cared for* — never plastic, never flat, never pristine-perfect.

## §2 · The realism standard
- **PBR is mandatory** for physical objects: believable albedo, roughness, normal/detail, and —
  where true — metalness. A surface with no roughness/normal variation reads as plastic and is
  rejected.
- **Texture quality bar:** crisp at the distance the visitor sees it from; no muddy, stretched,
  low-res, or obviously-tiling textures in focal areas. Resolution serves *viewing distance* —
  hero/foreground surfaces demand more than distant background.
- **No untextured flat-color surfaces** on physical props (exception: deliberate stylised
  elements like glow runes/UI-adjacent pieces, which follow §6).

## §3 · Story in the surface (the soul of the World)
Per Bible (*humane, handcrafted, the World is tended*), materials must carry **age and use**:
- Wood: grain, weathering, soft edges, warmth.
- Stone: moss, lichen, water-staining, irregularity.
- Metal: patina, wear at contact points, no chrome-perfect shine.
- Foliage: natural color variation, translucency in light, asymmetry.
- Fabric/paper: softness, slight wear, hand-made imperfection.
A brand-new, flawless surface is *wrong* unless newness is the point — the World looks lived-in
and loved.

## §4 · The material vocabulary (the World's palette of surfaces)
- **Warm wood** — the signature material (fences, signs, structures). The recurring "handmade"
  note across the World.
- **Mossy/weathered stone** — permanence, age, the natural world reclaiming.
- **Worn metal** — tools, mechanisms, gates; competence and use.
- **Living foliage** — health, growth, the forest's life.
- **Soft water** — flow, life, time (river, waterworks).
- **Warm glass/ceramic & paper** — domestic warmth (windows, lanterns, the workshop).
Consistency of this vocabulary across zones is what makes the World feel like *one place by one
maker.* New materials must justify themselves against this palette.

## §5 · Shared-material discipline (canon — quality *and* performance)
- **Reuse materials aggressively.** A small, curated set of shared materials per role/zone — not
  a unique material per object. This is both a performance law and a *coherence* law: shared
  materials make the World feel unified.
- New unique materials are the exception, justified by a hero moment, never the default.

## §6 · Emissive surfaces (the glow rule, material side)
- Glow is delivered by **emissive materials**, warm and subtle (see LIGHTING §4).
- Emissive surfaces register with the **Day/Night system**: low intensity by day, raised at
  night so they become the night's focal lights — without adding real lights.
- Emissive must read as *natural energy* (firelight, lanternlight, living luminescence), never
  neon, never UI-glow.

## §7 · Per-zone material mood
- **Village:** warm wood, cozy stone, glowing glass — domestic, cared-for.
- **Forest:** living foliage, mossy stone, warm wood, soft water — wild but tended.
- **Mountains:** colder stone, worn metal, thinner materials — serious, earned.
- **Projects:** each world may assert material identity, within the realism standard (§2).
- **Hero House:** the warmest materials — hearth, soft wood, glowing windows.

## §8 · What BELONGS / what does NOT
**Belongs:** weathered PBR wood with grain; mossy stone with lichen; patinated metal; foliage
with color variation and translucency; warm glowing glass; shared, coherent material sets;
subtle warm emissive.

**Does NOT belong:** flat single-color untextured props; plastic/chrome-perfect shine; muddy,
stretched, or visibly-tiling textures in focal areas; pristine factory-new surfaces where wear
is expected; neon emissive; a unique bespoke material for every object; materials that clash
with the warm-handmade vocabulary.

## §9 · Evaluation checklist (for agents — "does this material belong?")
1. **PBR realism** — proper albedo/roughness/normal; not plastic/flat? (§2)
2. **Texture quality** — crisp for its viewing distance; no muddy/stretched/obvious-tile? (§2)
3. **Story in surface** — shows believable age/use/care, not factory-new? (§3)
4. **Vocabulary fit** — belongs to the warm wood/stone/metal/foliage/water palette? (§4)
5. **Shared-material discipline** — reuses existing materials where possible? (§5)
6. **Emissive correctness** — warm, subtle, day/night-aware, not neon/UI? (§6)
7. **Zone mood fit** — matches the zone's material mood? (§7)
